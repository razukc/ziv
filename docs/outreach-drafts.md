# Outreach drafts — accessibility organizations

Permission-first outreach letters. One generic template plus three tailored
for the organizations whose published work Ziv's documentation already cites.
Read the usage rules at the bottom before sending anything.

The core principle of every letter here: **you ask for their permission and
their time, not theirs for yours.** Where Ziv's manners come from their
published practice, the letter is a credit-note, not a pitch.

## Generic template

**Subject:** A hackathon prototype built on your published practice — may we share what we made?

Dear [Name / team name],

I'm [name], [one-line affiliation, e.g. "a solo developer," or skip
entirely if you're not affiliated]. At the end of September we finished a
hackathon prototype — not a product, and not a company — called **Ziv**: a
small program that plays a message as braille-rhythm vibration on an Android
phone, with no audio and no spoken reply. Every message is framed by cues: an
arrival signal, working taps while the turn runs, and a closing pattern when
it ends.

We want to be clear about where those "manners" came from: they were
**adopted from your published early-years deafblind practice**
[Sense UK/Insight — cite the specific resource], not invented. That naming
appears in our description, in the code's documentation, and in the README.

We're writing not to promote anything — the prototype isn't validated with
DeafBlind readers, we're not fundraising or selling, and we don't expect a
partnership. We're asking **your advice, and your permission, before we do
anything more**: if you're open to it, we could send a 2-minute video and a
written description first, entirely at your discretion, and you'd tell us
whether we should stop here or keep going.

If you'd prefer we not proceed, we'll take that as its answer and stop — no
follow-up.

With thanks for your published work,
[Name] · [email] · [repo link]

## Sense UK (and the Insight-published resource) — the credit-letter

**Recipient:** the Information and Advice team, via the contact form at
<sense.org.uk/contact-us> (the live route; older listings show
`info@sense.org.uk` — prefer the form). Optionally cc the same request to the
publisher via insightdeafblind.org.

**Subject:** Thank you — and asking permission, before anything: a hackathon prototype built on your published practice

Dear Information and Advice team,

I'm [name], a solo developer. Last month our hackathon prototype — **Ziv**,
a small program that plays a message as braille-rhythm vibration on an
Android phone, with no audio output and no spoken reply — was submitted. I'm
writing about one thing before anything else: **the channel's manners were
adopted from your practice**, not invented by us. The published resource
"How to support a child with deafblindness in their early years"
(<insightdeafblind.org/resource/how-to-support-a-child-with-deafblindness-in-their-early-years/>)
gave us the four rules the code now enforces: every event is cued before it
happens, cues mark start and end, waiting stays legible, and nothing barges
in. Our documentation names Sense UK as the source in the README, the
description, and the code comments.

The prototype is not validated with DeafBlind readers, is not a product, and
we are not fundraising or selling. We're asking only whether you'd be willing
to receive — at your discretion, no obligation and with no follow-up pressure
from us — a two-minute video and a one-page description, so that if we ever
say "this built on your work" publicly, it's after you've seen it and know
exactly what is being described. If you'd rather we proceed no further, one
short reply is enough and we'll stop here.

With real thanks for the work we learned from,
[Name] · [email] · [repo link]

Dear IBTC team,

missing: our honest claim is "a working prototype of a new channel" — **we

The only ask: **may we send a two-minute video and a one-page description to
this address**, at your discretion and with zero obligation? If anything in
the assessment seems wrong — the close, the 530–770 ms per-letter pacing, the

next. We want only decisions from experts, taken at the earliest stage they
would still change the route, and we'll accept no as an answer — before yes,
or after.

Yours,
[Name] · [email] · [repo link]

## Usage rules

1. **Send order = warmth order.** Sense UK is the credit-letter (lowest
   friction; you're thanking them) → HKNC data-letter (license politeness) →
   NFB review-letter (highest friction — they're asked to evaluate).
   Staggering the sends avoids three people answering at once.
2. **One ask, one inbox, one reminder.** After 2–3 weeks without a reply,
   one short reminder to the *same address*, then stop. Never follow up
   silence with a *new* message to a different route at the same org —
   non-reply from a gated community service is an answer.
3. **Never ask** for participants, client lists, introductions to their
   community, or marketing channels. The only ask in every letter is
   *receiving a video and granting permission*. If they offer a demo call,
   it's on their schedule. If they suggest a contact within the org, ask
   whether it's all right to reach out yourself rather than routing around.
4. **Before sending any of them**, confirm the repo README and the demo
   video are public-facing, since each letter names a repo link they may
   open on their own.
5. **Contact routes verified 2026-11:** Sense's live route is the webform
   (the email addresses circulating on other sites are older listings) —
   prefer the form; HKNC `hkncinfo@hknc.org` is the listed general inbox,
   with `info@hknc.org` reachable via helenkeller.org; NFB's `access@nfb.org`
   is the IBTC's published address for questions of this kind. Re-verify
   before an actual send if any of these age.
